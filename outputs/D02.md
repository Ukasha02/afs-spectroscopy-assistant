## Recommendation

### 1. NIR spectroscopy — Diffuse reflection (Reflex probe) (`primary`, cost average, confidence high)
- *Spectral range (nominal):* 780-2500 nm (nominal method range)
- Inline NIR reflection is specifically recommended in AFS dairy applications for opaque or highly concentrated dairy products such as whole milk concentrates, yogurt, or butter. `[source: S01 p.17]`
- Diffuse reflection probe geometry handles scattering media and powders with an optical penetration depth of 0.5-1 mm at average cost and average selectivity. `[source: S01 Table 1 (p.6)]`
- Operates with silica fiber optic cables that allow robust remote connection between the process line and the analyzer. `[source: S01 p.5; S02 p.7]`
- *Condition:* Requires chemometric calibration (e.g., PLS regression with MSC or SNV scatter correction) to decouple chemical fat/protein absorption from physical scatter variations caused by texture and fat globule size. `[source: S01 p.17]`
- *Condition:* Temperature variations must be systematically incorporated into the calibration matrix to prevent thermal spectral shifts from confounding composition predictions. `[assumption]`
- *Range note:* 780-2500 nm is the nominal NIR method range; specific informative sub-bands (such as 850-1100 nm or 1200-2100 nm) will be established during laboratory screening. `[source: S01 p.10; S02 p.12; S02 p.18]`
- *Confidence:* Directly matches documented dairy PAT use cases in S01 p.17 and worked example D02.

### 2. NIR spectroscopy — Transflection (TransFlex probe) (`alternative`, cost average, confidence medium)
- *Spectral range (nominal):* 780-2500 nm (nominal method range)
- In dairy applications, a transflection probe can in some cases serve as a versatile combined solution working across both low-fat and high-fat containing milk streams. `[source: S01 p.18]`
- *Why not simpler:* For dense, highly opaque concentrated products, optical transmission paths can saturate or foul more easily than a flush-mounted diffuse reflection interface. `[source: S01 p.18]`
- *Condition:* To be evaluated during lab screening with variable pathlength gaps (e.g., 1-10 mm) to check whether light penetration is sufficient without signal clipping. `[source: S01 Table 1 (p.6)]`
- *Confidence:* Transflection feasibility depends heavily on the exact optical density and thickness of the concentrated product.

## Measurement concept

- **Probe:** Diffuse reflection probe (e.g., Reflex / DRS probe with sapphire window) · **Geometry:** Diffuse reflection flush-mounted into the pipe wall · **Location:** Processing pipe section with stable flow and minimal aeration · **Architecture:** undecided
- **Fiber link:** Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or metal-coated) (compatible: True)
- Flush mounting a reflection probe into a pipe or vat allows direct optical interaction with the flowing medium while minimizing flow disturbance and dead legs. `[source: S01 p.17]`
- Vis-NIR silica fibers transmit from 350 nm to 2400 nm, covering the primary NIR vibrational overtone region required for diffuse reflection. `[source: S02 p.6]`
- Silica fiber cables can run 100-300 m, enabling remote installation of the analyzer away from wet production environments if needed. `[source: S02 p.7]`
- Architecture (centralized multiplexed spectrometer vs. distributed spectral IoT sensors) is left undecided until the number of monitoring points, distance, and budget are clarified. `[source: S02 p.18]`

## Risks and uncertainty

- **[technical]** Variations in product temperature and texture (particle/globule size) induce baseline shifts and scattering changes that mimic composition changes. → *Mitigation:* Apply mathematical scatter corrections (MSC or SNV) and systematically incorporate the full temperature and texture range into the multivariate calibration training set. `[source: S01 p.17]`
- **[technical]** Fouling or milk stone deposition on the probe optical window over time causing measurement drift. → *Mitigation:* Evaluate flush-mounted sanitary sapphire window designs, CIP cleanability, and periodic automated reference checks. `[assumption]`
- **[assumption]** Optical model accuracy is inherently bounded by the precision and repeatability of the client's laboratory reference assay. → *Mitigation:* Quantify reference method standard error of laboratory analysis (SEL) prior to calibration modeling. `[assumption]`
- **[evidence_scope]** Published dairy PAT examples demonstrate technical feasibility in principle, not guaranteed quantitative performance on this specific proprietary formulation. → *Mitigation:* Conduct a structured laboratory screening on actual client samples before specifying commercial production hardware. `[source: DOCX §3]`

**Assumptions**

- The product flows continuously under pressure without severe entrained air bubbles or voids at the pipe wall.
- The optical interface must meet standard food-grade clean-in-place (CIP) hygienic design principles.
- Standard offline laboratory assays (e.g., Gerber/Rose-Gottlieb for fat, Kjeldahl/combustion for protein) are available to provide reference numbers for calibration.
- A single diffuse reflection NIR measurement will suffice without requiring secondary optical techniques, unless lab screening proves inadequate.

## Feasibility plan

**Objective:** Determine the feasibility and accuracy of in-line NIR spectroscopy for continuous fat and protein monitoring across product temperature and texture variations, benchmarked against current laboratory assays.

- Methods screened in the lab: NIR diffuse reflection, NIR transflection
- Methods compared: NIR diffuse reflection, NIR transflection

**1. Lab screening**
- *samples:* Collect representative batch samples of the opaque concentrated milk product spanning normal and boundary variations in fat, protein, and texture. `[assumption]`
- *establish:* Screen samples on benchtop spectrometers to establish optimal spectral range, optical pathlength/penetration, and spectral sensitivity to fat and protein concentrations. `[source: S02 p.20]`

**2. Method comparison against the reference**
- *reference method:* Pair each recorded optical spectrum with duplicate results from the client's established laboratory reference methods for fat and protein, maintaining strict sample time-alignment. `[assumption]`
- *compare on:* Compare diffuse reflection versus transflection geometry on prediction error (RMSEP), bias, and linearity under controlled temperature sweeps mimicking plant variations. `[assumption]`
- *decision basis:* Base the go/no-go decision on whether single-method prediction error meets client-defined operational precision targets on an independent validation set; no performance figures are invented. `[source: DOCX §2 D02]`

**3. In-line / in-situ trial**
- *location:* Install a trial probe in an accessible processing line port where the concentrated product is well-mixed and free of air pockets. `[assumption]`
- *interface:* Fit a sanitary flush-mounted probe (diffuse reflection or selected geometry) directly into the pipe wall. `[source: S01 p.17]`
- *verify:* Collect continuous in-line spectra during dynamic production runs, pulling time-stamped grab samples for reference laboratory testing to verify real-time model tracking. `[assumption]`

**4. Scale-up**
- *robustness:* Verify long-term baseline stability, signal reproducibility, and resistance to line vibration and thermal cycling. `[assumption]`
- *cleaning sterilization:* Test probe cleanability and seal durability during standard production CIP/SIP wash cycles. `[assumption]`
- *industry standards:* Ensure optical probe materials and process connections conform to food safety and dairy hygienic design standards. `[assumption]`
- *few wavelength simplification:* Evaluate whether the full-spectrum chemometric model can be reduced to a dedicated few-wavelength optical sensor (e.g., LED or filtered photodiode sensor) for commercial line deployment. `[source: S01 p.9; S02 p.4; S02 p.12]`
