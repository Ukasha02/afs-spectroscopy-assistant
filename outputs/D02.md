### Recommendation

- **Premise: Spectroscopy can give earlier information about fat and protein composition changes in this product.** — plausible to be tested. An inline NIR reflection application for fat and protein in milk is described, as a reason to test rather than proof for this product [S01 p.17]. Accuracy and response for this concentrated product are unknown until measured against the laboratory reference. *(assumption)*
- **NIR spectroscopy (Diffuse reflection probe, flush-mounted in the line).** The dairy example supports inline NIR reflection for opaque or highly concentrated products such as whole milk concentrates [S01 p.17]. Reflection is sensitive to surface characteristics and fat globule size, so these factors should be checked on this product [S01 p.17].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transflection probe (comparison candidate)).** The dairy example notes that a transflection probe may suit both low-fat and high-fat milk products [S01 p.18]. Transmission fails when milk is too thick or fat-rich, so transflection must be tested against reflection on this product [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** Diffuse reflection probe at the flowing product as primary candidate; transflection probe as comparison if a light path is feasible [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.17]. Measuring point not yet known; flush mounting into the pipe or vat follows the dairy example. *(assumption)* Vis-NIR silica fibers transmit about 350-2400 nm, so the upper end of the 780-2500 nm NIR range may not be covered; link length is undefined [S02 p.6; S01 p.10]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and flush-mounting for the pipe or vat, Contact, fouling and cleaning compatibility, Optical configuration for reflection and transflection, Analyzer coverage up to 2.5 um and fiber length, Distance to analyzer and access points.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change in formulation or matrix can invalidate a fat or protein calibration [DOCX §5].
- Morphological factors such as inhomogeneity or process turbulence must be considered, since scattering overlaps with other phenomena [S01 p.7].
- Probe window fouling is unassessed and could drift the signal. *(assumption)*
- Neither NIR reflection nor a transflection comparison is guaranteed to give earlier composition information. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect samples across the agreed fat and protein range and the temperature and texture variation of production.
2. Record reflection and transflection spectra from each candidate, with independent laboratory reference measurements and timing information.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out production runs before any combination is considered.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against the client's accuracy and response-time requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree accuracy, bias and response-time criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent production runs.
