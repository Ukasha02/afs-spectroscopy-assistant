# D02 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Provisional feasibility assessment. Provisional feasibility assessment for in-line fat and protein monitoring in opaque concentrated milk.

**Requirements stated.** Opaque, concentrated milk product in a flowing line; Fat and protein are the target components; Product temperature and texture vary during production; Retain existing laboratory checks while seeking earlier information.

**Key unknowns.** Product composition ranges and target accuracy; Operating temperature, pressure, flow rate, and pipe geometry; CIP cleaning regime and sanitary interface requirements; Reference assay uncertainty, turnaround delay, and sampling time.

### Recommendation

- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection probe).** The supplied dairy example supports NIR reflection for opaque or concentrated dairy products [S01 p.17]. Physical scattering and fat globule size strongly affect reflection signals and require chemometric scatter correction [S01 p.17].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** Transflection probes can serve as combined solutions across varying fat contents [S01 p.18]. Transmission fails when the product is too opaque or fat-rich for light to penetrate [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** Flush-mounted diffuse reflection probe in the pipe wall interacting directly with the medium [S01 p.17]. Transflection probe evaluated in laboratory screening as a comparison geometry. Mounted directly in the production processing line where milk flows continuously. Vis-NIR silica fiber link connecting the probe to the spectrometer [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Sanitary probe mechanical mounting and seal compatibility (CIP/SIP resistance), Probe optical window geometry to prevent product accumulation, Fiber cable routing constraints between pipeline and analyzer.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Variations in physical scattering and fat globule size alter the reflection spectrum and can mimic composition changes [S01 p.17].
- Broad overlapping NIR absorption bands require chemometrics, and models are not yet calibrated for this stream [S01 p.10].
- Concentrated dairy streams can foul the optical window and distort spectral measurements. *(assumption)*
- An optical probe is not guaranteed to achieve the required accuracy without empirical testing. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, sanitary interface ratings, and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect representative product samples across agreed fat and protein ranges and production variations.
2. Record spectra from each candidate together with independent reference laboratory assays and timing information.
3. Build single-method chemometric models first, applying scatter correction, and validate on independent batches.
4. Compare model prediction error, bias, and stability against the client's operational requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical accuracy and prediction error criteria with the client prior to testing, and choose the simplest optical interface that meets these targets on independent validation batches.
