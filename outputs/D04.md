### Recommendation

- **Premise: An in situ pilot can track glucose and lactate promptly in the culture.** — plausible to be tested. A bioprocess example uses Raman in one reactor for glucose and lactate [S01 p.16]. A published application is a reason to test, not proof for this customer [DOCX §3].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman fiber probe (immersion to confirm)).** A bioprocess example uses Raman in one reactor for glucose and lactate; Raman suits aqueous media [S01 p.16]. The Raman signal is weak and fluorescence can dominate; check signal quality first [S01 p.11].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe).** The cited bioprocess figure lists glucose and lactate as NIR-accessible variables [S01 p.17]. NIR may lack sensitivity for trace compounds below 0.1 percent [S01 p.16].
- **NIR and Raman combination (Combined probe or two probes at one spot).** NIR with Raman gave the best online result in a fluidized-bed pellet coating process [S01 p.7]. Only after each method is assessed alone; whether it adds value here is a hypothesis to test.

**Measurement concept.** Raman probe; table lists liquid, powder and solid samples at 2-5 mm optical path; immersion to be confirmed [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Bioreactor measuring point not yet defined by the client. *(assumption)* Vis-NIR silica transmits 350-2400 nm; Raman band coverage depends on laser wavelength, unconfirmed; no link length defined [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type and sterilizability, laser excitation and optics, installation and analyzer distance.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]; Raman spectroscopy: Raman shift (cm-1); signal recorded in 400-1100 nm nominal [S01 p.11] (Excitation wavelength not yet set; state separately from Raman shift). Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Medium and cell-population changes can invalidate a calibration built for one state [DOCX §5].
- Cell scattering and turbidity can overlap with the analyte signal [S01 p.7].
- No single method or combination is guaranteed to track these analytes [S01 p.16].
- Accuracy, response time, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect culture samples across the agreed glucose and lactate range and cell-density changes over runs.
2. Record spectra from each candidate with time-stamped independent glucose and lactate reference results.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out runs before any combined model.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against the client's requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree error, bias and response-time limits with the client before the pilot; choose the simplest method meeting them on independent runs.
