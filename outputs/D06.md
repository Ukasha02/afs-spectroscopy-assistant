### Recommendation

- **Premise: Fiber measurement near the process notices water changes sooner than the lab.** — plausible to be tested. Probes analyse at a distance, without sampling, in-process [S01 p.10]. Response speed for petroleum water is untested. *(assumption)*
- **Premise: Analyzer outside the process can be linked to the probe by fiber.** — unverifiable. Silica suits NIR; mid-IR needs non-silica fiber [S02 p.6]. Fiber family and link length are not stated. *(assumption)*
- **MIR (FTIR) with ATR probe (ATR immersion probe (ZrO2 or diamond tip)).** Application example for water content in petroleum supports an MIR-ATR probe [S02 p.13]. Mid-IR needs PIR, CIR, hollow-waveguide or partly fluoride fiber; silica cannot carry it [S02 p.6].
- **NIR with transmission or transflection probe (Transmission or transflection probe, 1-10 mm path).** NIR is the most common fiber-probe online method; no petroleum water example in materials [S01 p.10]. Overlapping bands suggest chemometrics; water signal must be proven in this matrix [S01 p.10].

**Measurement concept.** MIR-ATR immersion probe (primary candidate) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. NIR transmission or transflection probe (comparison) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Near process; analyzer outside process area; in-line or bypass not stated. No link stated; mid-IR needs non-silica fiber [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. **Spectral range.** MIR (FTIR), ATR: 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 um) nominal [S01 p.9]; NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change of matrix or composition can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Process turbulence and sample inhomogeneity must be considered when choosing the technique [S01 p.7].
- Neither a single method nor a combination is guaranteed to help. *(general knowledge)*
- Accuracy, update rate and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Fiber family and link length unknown; mid-IR cannot pass silica fiber [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect petroleum samples across the agreed water range, batch, temperature and flow variation.
2. Record MIR-ATR and NIR spectra with timed laboratory water results.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out batches; combine only if justified.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow water changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree accuracy and response-time targets with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent batches.
