# D06 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Provisional feasibility assessment. Target (water) and matrix (petroleum stream) defined; level and appearance unknown.

**Requirements stated.** Water in petroleum stream, now measured in the lab; Measurement closer to the process for earlier change detection; Analyzer outside the process area; Clarify points and a first comparison before buying equipment.

**Key unknowns.** Water level (ppm or %) and change to detect; Stream appearance, phases, temperature, pressure, fouling; Lab reference method, accuracy, sampling frequency; Analyzer distance, existing fibers, response needs.

### Clarification questions

1. Which water range (ppm or %) must be detected, and is the stream clear, turbid or emulsified?
2. Which lab method measures water today, with what accuracy and sampling frequency?
3. How do temperature, pressure, composition and fouling vary at the sampling point?
4. What response time, accuracy and budget apply; how far is the analyzer, and are fibers present?
5. Which decision should faster data drive, and what does late detection cost today?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: A fiber-based measurement closer to the process will notice changes sooner than lab sampling.** — plausible to be tested. Online analysis removes sampling delay and contamination risk; speed for this stream is untested [S01 p.3].
- **MIR-ATR: IR spectroscopy with ATR probe (ATR immersion probe (ZrO2 or diamond tip)).** The water-in-petroleum example uses an ATR probe that tracks water absorbance [S02 p.13]. Mid-IR fiber such as PIR or CIR (or HWG or fluoride fibers, by range) is needed; ATR depth is only 1-6 um, so fouling needs checking [S02 p.6; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** The dairy example uses transmission for clear liquids; no petroleum example is in the materials [S01 p.18]. Suited only to clear liquids; NIR bands overlap and need chemometrics [S01 p.18; S01 p.10].

**Measurement concept.** MIR ATR immersion probe with the tip in the stream [S01 p.10; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Sampling point nearer the process; analyzer outside the process area. Mid-IR fiber (e.g. PIR or CIR) to the analyzer; link length not stated [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe tip and shaft, fiber type and link length, installation and fouling access.

**Spectral range.** MIR: 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 um) nominal [S01 p.9] (Usable band set by fiber and probe; PIR covers 3-17 um); NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Silica fiber reaches about 2.4 um; comparison only). Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change in stream composition can invalidate a water calibration [DOCX §5].
- Process turbulence or inhomogeneity must be considered when choosing the technique [S01 p.7].
- Neither a method nor a faster signal is guaranteed to improve control. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, response time and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Mid-IR link length and ratings are undefined; silica cannot carry mid-IR [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect stream samples across the realistic water and process range, with lab reference values.
2. Record MIR-ATR and NIR spectra from the same samples, each with an independent lab water reference and timing information.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out runs before any combination.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree accuracy, bias, response time and delay with the client before testing; choose the simplest setup meeting them on independent batches.
