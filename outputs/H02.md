### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** Laboratory protein and fat results can serve as reference for the pilot [S01 p.17].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection (milk concentrate)).** The dairy example supports NIR reflection for opaque concentrates [S01 p.17]. Sensitive to surface and fat globule size; scatter must be handled [S01 p.17].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection (whey)).** The dairy example names NIR transmission as preferred for whey [S01 p.18]. Pathlength must be matched to the stream in lab tests [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transflection (single interface for both streams)).** Transflection may serve both low- and high-fat products [S01 p.18]. Holds only in some cases; compare with stream-specific interfaces [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe for the concentrate; NIR transmission or transflection probe for whey; transflection may be a combined option for both, to be verified [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.18]. Compare interfaces on lab samples first, then flush-mount in pipe or vat per stream [S01 p.17]. No link stated; silica fibers cover UV/Vis and NIR [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type per stream, Transflection pathlength, Installation and cleaning compatibility.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal; usable bands from sample spectra). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Whey and concentrate may need separate calibrations [S01 p.18].
- Fat globule size and scattering can mimic composition changes [S01 p.17].
- No interface or added method is guaranteed to help. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Contact interfaces may foul; cleaning regime unknown. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect whey and concentrate samples across client-agreed composition ranges, temperature, homogenisation and bubble variation.
2. Record spectra with each interface alongside time-aligned laboratory protein and fat results.
3. Build single-interface models per stream first; validate on held-out runs or batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against the client's requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest interface set meeting them on independent runs.
