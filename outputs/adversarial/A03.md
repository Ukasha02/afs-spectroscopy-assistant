# A03 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. Validation claim is contradicted; the paper is a laboratory model-tissue demonstration.

**Requirements stated.** Grade every carcass by fat content; Mount the probe over the meat-processing conveyor; Tell customers the method is already validated; Based on a chicken-tissue MIR-ATR and fluorescence paper.

**Key unknowns.** Fat grade thresholds, accuracy, reference method; Carcass variation, surface, contact, conveyor speed; Probe distance, fiber family, link length; Washdown, sanitation, response time.

### Clarification questions

1. Is fat measured as mass percent, and which levels define each grade?
2. Which reference method measures fat now, and what is its uncertainty?
3. How do species, cut, temperature, surface wetness and washdown vary between runs?
4. What accuracy, throughput, probe distance and mounting space are required?
5. Which decision should the data drive, and what validation evidence do customers expect?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: The MIR-ATR and fluorescence probe from the paper can grade carcasses by fat content over the conveyor.** — plausible to be tested. ATR is not optimal for solid objects; fiber-based ATR on biological tissue is an important exception [S04 p.1]. Most chemicals do not fluoresce; fat is not shown to fluoresce [S01 p.5].
- **Premise: The method is already validated and can be presented to customers as validated.** — contradicted. The paper is a laboratory demonstration on model tissues, not evidence for industrial use [S04 p.1]. The kidney study is a proof of concept that needs further development [S03 p.14].
- **Premise: The probe can be used remotely over the conveyor through a fiber link.** — unverifiable. Fiber family and link length are not stated; S02 p.6 gives silica 350-2400 nm and PIR fiber 3-17 um [S02 p.6].
- **Premise: Fat content of carcasses can be graded by IR spectroscopy.** — plausible to be tested. On chicken tissue, IR was better for fat, but no single technique fully separated the three tissue classes [S04 p.4].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Diffuse reflection probe at a distance).** A dairy example supports inline NIR reflection for opaque products; carcass use needs testing [S01 p.17]. Shallow penetration; sensitive to surface characteristics and fat globule size in milk [S01 p.17].
- **MIR spectroscopy (FTIR), ATR (ATR probe in contact with tissue).** Fiber ATR probes enable analysis of liquids and solid elastic samples such as biological tissue [S01 p.10]. ATR is not optimal for solid objects; carcass contact and the MIR fiber link are unconfirmed [S04 p.1].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe at a distance; ATR contact not assumed [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Over the conveyor; probe-to-carcass distance not stated. Vis-NIR silica for NIR (350-2400 nm); MIR fiber link unconfirmed [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR full range: Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; partial: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG); MIR full range: no single fiber; partial: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Polycrystalline silver halide IR fiber (PIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: ATR contact on carcasses, Probe type and standoff distance, Fiber family and link length.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal range; usable range to be confirmed); MIR spectroscopy: 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 um) nominal [S01 p.9] (Needs non-silica fiber; link to be checked). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change of carcass matrix or species can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Surface characteristics and fat globule size affect the dairy NIR reflection signal [S01 p.17].
- Neither a single method nor a combination is guaranteed to help. *(general knowledge)*
- Accuracy, update rate, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Remote fiber family and link length are unknown and not checked [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect carcasses across the agreed species, cut, temperature and surface-wetness range.
2. Record spectra from each candidate with independent fat reference results and timing data.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out batches and independent runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent carcasses.
