- **Premise: UV/Vis on the filler can reliably identify off-taste batches and reject them automatically.** — unverifiable. No target compound, reference method or decision limit exists yet. *(assumption)*
- **UV/Vis transmission or transflection probe, after lab screening (Transmission or transflection, 1-10 mm path).** Clear, low-density liquids suit transmission probes: 'For highly transparent liquids with low optical density, UV-Transmission or Transflection probes are ideal.' [S01 p.16]. Table 1 rates this probe low in sensitivity and selectivity; the juice target must be confirmed first [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR transmission or transflection probe (Transmission or transflection).** The dairy example prefers transmission for clear liquids: 'NIR Transmission is the preferred method for transparent or semi-transparent liquids.' [S01 p.18]. The example is milk, not juice; NIR bands overlap and need chemometrics [S01 p.18; S01 p.10].

### Risks and uncertainty

- Calibrations are matrix-specific; a recipe change requires re-checking them [DOCX §5].
- Process turbulence and sample inhomogeneity must be considered and may affect readings [S01 p.7].
- No method is guaranteed to detect off taste; a spectral signal may be unrelated to taste. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, response time, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- The cited examples are bioprocess and milk media; none covers juice. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen good and off-taste juice batches with UV/Vis, NIR and Raman to find a target and usable range.
2. Record spectra with independent sensory or GC/HPLC reference results, timed to the sampling point.
3. Build single-method models first; combine only if justified, validated on held-out batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client-agreed requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree accuracy, false-reject rate and response time before testing; pick the simplest configuration meeting them on independent batches.
