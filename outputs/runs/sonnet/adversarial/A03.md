# A03 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. A laboratory chicken-tissue demonstration does not validate carcass grading on a conveyor.

**Requirements stated.** Mount the paper's MIR-ATR plus fluorescence probe over a meat conveyor; Grade every carcass by fat content; Tell customers the method is validated.

**Key unknowns.** Fat range, unit, grade thresholds; Carcass surface, contact, probe distance; Reference method and accuracy; Accuracy, response time, sanitation.

### Clarification questions

1. What fat range, unit and thresholds must be graded, and which carcass types and regions are measured?
2. Which reference method gives fat, with what uncertainty, and can timed paired samples be taken?
3. How do skin, moisture, blood, temperature and presentation vary along the line?
4. Can a probe touch the carcass, and what conveyor speed, cleaning and sanitation apply?
5. What decision should the grade drive, and what accuracy and budget apply?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: The method is already validated and can be stated as such to customers.** — contradicted. The paper is a laboratory demonstration on model chicken tissue; a published application is a reason to test, not proof [S04 p.3; DOCX §3].
- **Premise: The combined MIR-ATR and fluorescence probe will grade carcasses by fat content on the conveyor.** — plausible to be tested. The paper separated three chicken tissue types qualitatively (PCA), not fat content quantitatively on carcasses [S04 p.4]. ATR needs tight contact and has a 1-6 um optical path; combining this with an overhead non-contact setup looks hard, which is an inference, not a stated fact [S04 p.4; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Fluorescence needs a fluorescent component; whether one tracks fat in carcasses is unknown [S04 p.4].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Diffuse reflection, surface at a distance (non-contact)).** NIR diffuse reflectance allows measurement without direct contact, at a distance; testing it first is an assumption [S01 p.10]. No carcass example exists in the materials; the calibration is matrix-specific and must be built and tested [S01 p.10].
- **MIR-ATR + fluorescence (paper's probe) (ATR contact with fluorescence at the same spot).** The paper shows complementary information from one spot on model chicken tissue, so it is a lab comparison candidate [S04 p.4]. It needs tight contact; benefit over the best single method is a hypothesis to test [S04 p.4].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection at a distance; the paper's ATR plus fluorescence probe needs contact [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Over the conveyor, as stated by the client; distance and contact unknown. The chart lists mid-IR fiber types beyond silica; fiber link and analyzer distance are not given. Fit of nominal method ranges to fiber ranges is an assumption [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um); FLUO: UV-Vis silica covers the nominal range [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type, Installation and distance, Optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal, not instrument range); MIR: 4000-400 cm-1 nominal [S01 p.9] (Nominal; fiber must be checked). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A chicken-tissue lab study cannot support a customer validation claim [DOCX §3].
- A change of carcass type or composition can invalidate a calibration [S01 p.10].
- Moisture, temperature and scattering can mimic fat changes. *(general knowledge)*
- Contact ATR on carcasses raises fouling and sanitation issues. *(assumption)*
- No method or combination is guaranteed to help. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, interface ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect carcass samples across the agreed fat range and surface, temperature and presentation variation.
2. Record spectra from each candidate with independent fat references and timing.
3. Build single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent material.
