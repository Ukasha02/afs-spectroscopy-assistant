**Requirements stated.** Coloured by-product followed in a fermenter by UV/Vis absorbance; Spectrometer to move to control room; Leftover chalcogenide IR fiber as probe link.

**Key unknowns.** By-product, concentration level, absorption band; Broth turbidity and biomass; Fiber length and distance; Cleaning or sterilization, reference method.

### Recommendation

- **Premise: The leftover chalcogenide IR fiber can link the probe to a UV/Vis spectrometer in the control room** — contradicted. Chalcogenide As-S fiber transmits about 1.1-6.5 um, so it does not cover UV/Vis; a UV-Vis silica fiber is needed [S02 p.6].
- **Premise: Moving the spectrometer to the control room via fiber is feasible** — plausible to be tested. Silica cables of 100-300 m exist for remote process control; the actual distance is unknown [S02 p.7].
- **Premise: The old fiber's length and condition are adequate** — unverifiable. Length and end-face condition are not stated, so the link is undefined. *(assumption)*
- **UV/Vis absorbance (Transmission or transflection probe on UV-Vis silica fiber).** UV/Vis is described as a low-cost, robust tool in bioreactor monitoring [S01 p.16]. Turbid broth can saturate transmission, so geometry needs lab confirmation [S01 p.16].
- **Fluorescence (Fluorescence fiber probe on UV-Vis silica fiber).** Fluorimetry is a key method for online analysis of biotechnological processes [S01 p.12]. It needs a fluorescent species, as most chemicals do not fluoresce [S01 p.5].

**Measurement concept.** Transmission or transflection probe; ATR if broth is too opaque [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Probe in fermenter, point undefined; spectrometer in control room. Chalcogenide fiber does not cover UV/Vis; use UV-Vis silica fiber. Length unknown [S02 p.6]. Rule check of the stated fiber: UVVIS: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR) none; FLUO: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR) none [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type, Fiber type and length, Sterilization compatibility.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible; depends on target and setup nominal [S01 p.5] (Band found in lab screening). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Chalcogenide fiber would pass no UV/Vis light [S02 p.6].
- A change of broth composition can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Turbidity, biomass and temperature can mimic composition changes. *(general knowledge)*
- Fluorescence is not guaranteed to improve on UV/Vis. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, response time and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect broth samples across the agreed by-product range and real turbidity, biomass and temperature variation.
2. Record UV/Vis spectra, and fluorescence if justified, with independent references and timing.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on independent runs or batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements over a silica link.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
