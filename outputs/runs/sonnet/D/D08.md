### Recommendation

- **Premise: MIR-ATR and fluorescence together help classify heterogeneous tissue** — plausible to be tested. A lab chicken-tissue demonstration found fused data separated all three tissues; model-tissue study, not proof for this group's samples [S04 p.4; S02 p.15].
- **Premise: Same-spot measurement is needed for heterogeneous tissue** — plausible to be tested. The materials state that heterogeneous samples need information from the same small area, hard with alternating probes [S01 p.8; S04 p.2].
- **Premise: Fluorescence carries class-relevant information in these tissues** — unverifiable. Fluorimetry responds to emission of certain, mostly biological, substances; which fluorophores matter here is not yet known [S01 p.5].
- **MIR-ATR (ATR probe, direct contact with tissue).** The chicken fat, breast and liver demonstration used ATR IR, which separated fat well [S04 p.4]. ATR samples only a few micrometres and needs tight contact, so surface state must be tested [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Fluorescence (Fluorescence fiber probe).** In the same study fluorescence was better suited to recognising liver [S04 p.4]. Selectivity is rated low, and only fluorescent components are seen [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **MIR-ATR + fluorescence (Combi probe, same measurement spot).** The model-tissue study found the concatenated data revealed three distinct groups [S04 p.4]. Only after each method is assessed alone; a hypothesis to test [S01 p.7].

**Measurement concept.** ATR probe plus fluorescence probe, ideally a combi probe sharing the ATR crystal [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.2]. Bench laboratory, tissue pressed on the crystal. *(assumption)* MIR needs mid-IR fiber (CIR, PIR or HWG), not silica; fiber type and link unknown [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: MIR full range: no single fiber; partial: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Polycrystalline silver halide IR fiber (PIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; FLUO full range: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated); partial: Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Combi probe availability, Mid-IR fiber type, Optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** MIR: 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 um) nominal [S01 p.9] (Usable range depends on fiber and crystal); Fluorescence: UV/Vis excitation and emission nominal [S01 p.12] (Depends on fluorescent species). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Models from one animal lot or tissue state may not transfer. *(general knowledge)*
- Tissue inhomogeneity lowers reproducibility, notably for fat [S04 p.3].
- Combining methods is not guaranteed to beat the best single method [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, hardware compatibility and interface ratings are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Mid-IR fiber type and link are unknown; silica cannot carry MIR [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Prepare muscle, fat and liver from several independent animals across the agreed state and within-type variation, multiple points each.
2. Record MIR-ATR and fluorescence at the same points with independent tissue-identity reference and timing records.
3. Build single-method models first; fuse only if justified; validate on held-out animals or lots.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against requirements, per method versus fused.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent animals.
