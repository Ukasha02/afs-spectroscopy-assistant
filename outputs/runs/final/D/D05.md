### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** Rising optical density of the broth can serve as the biomass/growth signal [S01 p.16].
- **Premise unverifiable:** An optical signal will reveal growth changes earlier.
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** Biomass and metabolic signals together describe the run [S01 p.7].
- **UV/Vis spectroscopy (turbidity) (transmission or transflection probe).** Bioprocess example supports low-cost turbidity for biomass trends [S01 p.16]. Dense broth can saturate transmission; UV-ATR is then used [S01 p.16].
- **Fluorescence spectroscopy (fluorimetric probe).** Comparison for metabolic fluorophores such as NADH in bioprocesses [S01 p.16; S01 p.12]. Selectivity rated low; specificity must be checked [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** Used with fluorescence for yeast biomass; tracks major components such as glucose [S01 p.7; S01 p.16]. May lack sensitivity for trace compounds [S01 p.16].
- **NIR + fluorescence (two probes, time-aligned).** Yeast example showed complementary biomass and metabolite information [S01 p.7]. Only after single-method assessment; gain is a hypothesis to test [S01 p.7; S01 p.8].

**Measurement concept.** Transmission/transflection probe in the broth, UV-ATR if transmission saturates; fluorimetric probe for the metabolic candidate [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.16]. Fermenter-side probe assumed until installation and sterilization are stated. *(assumption)* No fiber link stated; silica fibers suit UV/Vis, NIR and Raman, while MIR needs IR fibers [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type and path length, sterilizable installation, optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]; Fluorescence: UV/Vis; depends on fluorophore nominal [S01 p.12]; NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Strain or medium changes can invalidate a calibration. *(general knowledge)*
- Rising turbidity and process turbulence can mimic or mask changes [S01 p.16; S01 p.7].
- Bioprocess examples are reasons to test, not proof for this broth. *(assumption)*
- Neither a method nor a combination is guaranteed to help [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, update rate, interface ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Sample several runs across growth phases, including medium, aeration, bubble and temperature variation.
2. Record UV/Vis, fluorescence and NIR spectra with time-aligned independent biomass and metabolite references.
3. Build single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out runs [S01 p.8].
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
