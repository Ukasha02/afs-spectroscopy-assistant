### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** In situ spectroscopy can give prompt glucose and lactate information that can be checked against the existing lab measurements [S01 p.16; S01 p.17].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman probe).** The bioprocess example supports Raman for glucose and lactate: "Raman is particularly well - suited for aqueous environments" [S01 p.16]. Check fluorescence background on real culture samples first [S01 p.11].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe).** Comparison candidate for major components: "NIR spectroscopy is effective for tracking major components like glucose or glycerol" [S01 p.16]. May lack sensitivity below 0.1 percent; test lactate at actual levels [S01 p.16].

**Measurement concept.** Raman probe (liquid samples, 2-5 mm path) and NIR transmission or transflection probe (liquid, colloidal, 1-10 mm path) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. In situ at a client-defined vessel port, time-aligned with reference sampling. *(assumption)* No link stated; silica fibers cover UV/Vis and NIR [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and sterilisability, Installation port in vessel, Optical configuration per method.

**Spectral range.** Raman spectroscopy: Raman shift in cm-1; recorded at 400–1100 nm nominal [S01 p.11] (Excitation wavelength to be defined separately); NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A calibration may not hold when medium or cells change. *(general knowledge)*
- Cell density and turbidity can mimic composition changes. *(general knowledge)*
- Neither candidate is guaranteed to meet required accuracy. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, sterilisation ratings, compatibility unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Cell deposits on the probe window may degrade signal. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Sample across agreed glucose and lactate ranges, run stages, cell densities and media.
2. Record Raman and NIR spectra with time-aligned lab reference values.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on independent runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before the test; choose the simplest configuration that meets them on independent runs.
