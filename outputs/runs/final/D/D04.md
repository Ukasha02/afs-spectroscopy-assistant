### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** An in situ spectroscopy pilot can track glucose and lactate and be checked against existing laboratory measurements [S01 p.16].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman fiber probe in the medium).** The bioprocess example names Raman for glucose and lactate: "Raman is particularly well - suited for aqueous environments, as it exhibits low absorbance for water molecules compared to infrared methods." [S01 p.16]. Fluorescence background may swamp the signal; check signal quality first [S01 p.11].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe).** Same example: "NIR spectroscopy is effective for tracking major components like glucose or glycerol" [S01 p.16]. NIR may lack sensitivity below 0.1 percent; lactate levels decide sufficiency [S01 p.16].

**Measurement concept.** Raman probe and NIR transmission or transflection probe for liquids, tested on real broth [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Culture vessel or loop, at a client-agreed port. No link stated. NIR: Vis-NIR silica reaches 2.4 um only, partial coverage. Raman: check fiber against the probe specification [S02 p.6; S01 p.10]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: RAMAN: Vis-NIR silica covers the nominal range; NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um) [S02 p.6; S01 p.11; S01 p.10]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and geometry, Sterilizable port installation, Optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** Raman spectroscopy: Raman shift in cm-1 nominal [S01 p.11] (Excitation wavelength to be selected; state separately); NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### First feasibility experiment

1. Sample across client-agreed glucose and lactate ranges, run phases, cell densities and medium variation.
2. Record Raman and NIR spectra time-aligned with lab glucose and lactate results.
3. Single-method models first, combination only if justified; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.
