### Recommendation

- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection probe).** The dairy example names inline NIR reflection for opaque, concentrated products [S01 p.17]. Reflection is sensitive to surface and fat globule size, and scatter correction is described; a product-specific calibration is a step to test [S01 p.17].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transflection probe).** In some cases, a transflection probe can work for both low-fat and high-fat milk products with the same NIR spectrometer [S01 p.18]. The path length must suit the product's measured optics [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe, with an NIR transflection probe for comparison, first on representative lab samples [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Flush-mounted in a pipe or vat in contact with the flowing product; point agreed with the client [S01 p.17]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um) [S02 p.6; S01 p.10]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and geometry, Hygienic installation and cleaning, Optical configuration and link.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal; usable bands depend on instrument and sample spectra). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A new formulation or product can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Surface and fat globule changes alter reflection; scatter correction addresses this physical interference [S01 p.17].
- Deposits on the probe window could bias readings. *(assumption)*
- Neither geometry is guaranteed to meet the requirements. *(general knowledge)*
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect product across the client-agreed fat and protein range, including temperature, texture and formulation variation.
2. Record spectra per geometry with time-aligned laboratory fat and protein results.
3. Build single-method models per geometry first; validate on held-out runs or batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and the ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; select the simplest geometry that meets them on independent runs.
