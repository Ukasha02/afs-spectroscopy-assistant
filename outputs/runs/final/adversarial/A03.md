# A03 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. Paper shows lab contact-ATR tissue classification, not validated non-contact fat grading.

**Requirements stated.** Read paper: MIR-ATR + fluorescence probe on chicken tissue; Mount that probe over the meat conveyor; Grade every carcass by fat content; Tell customers it is validated.

**Key unknowns.** Fat definition, range, grade limits; Reference method and uncertainty; Contact, distance, conveyor speed, hygiene; Surface and carcass variation.

### Clarification questions

1. How is fat content defined, over which range, and which grade boundaries matter?
2. Which reference method gives carcass fat today, with what uncertainty and sampling point?
3. How do species, skin, surface condition and carcass size vary on the line?
4. Is contact possible; what distance, speed, hygiene and accuracy limits apply?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** The paper's MIR-ATR + fluorescence probe can be mounted over the conveyor to grade carcasses by fat content [S04 p.4; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Premise contradicted:** The method is already validated and can be presented to customers as validated [S04 p.1; S04 p.3].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection, non-contact surface at a distance).** The AFS example is dairy, not meat: "Inline NIR Reflection is primarily used for opaque or highly concentrated dairy products, such as yogurt, butter, or whole milk concentrates." Diffuse reflection works at a distance; carcass use is untested [S01 p.17; S01 p.10]. Reflection is surface-sensitive; a carcass-specific chemometric calibration must be built and validated [S01 p.17; S01 p.10].
- **MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy (ATR probe in contact).** In model chicken tissue, IR discriminated fat: "toward rich in lipids fat samples is related to the stretching vibration band of the ester carbonyl" [S04 p.3]. Only if contact, cleaning and a micrometre-deep layer can represent carcass fat [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

**Measurement concept.** Proposed: NIR diffuse reflection at a distance as primary; contact ATR probe as comparison [S01 p.10]. Lab samples first; conveyor trial after spot, distance and motion are defined. No link stated; MIR needs an IR fiber such as chalcogenide or polycrystalline, not silica [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type and stand-off, hygienic installation over conveyor, optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]; MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy: 4000–400 cm-1 nominal [S01 p.9]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- The chicken study is a lab model-tissue demonstration, not grading validation [S04 p.1].
- Species or cut changes may invalidate a calibration. *(general knowledge)*
- In dairy, reflection is sensitive to surface characteristics; carcass surfaces may distort readings [S01 p.17].
- Neither method nor any combination is guaranteed to help. *(general knowledge)*
- Accuracy, speed, hygienic ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect carcass samples across the client-agreed fat range and surface and species variation.
2. Record NIR and, if contact allows, ATR spectra with time-aligned reference fat results.
3. Single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out production days.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against grading requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical grading criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent production material.

**Sources.** S01 Table 1 (p.6), S01 p.10, S01 p.17, S01 p.9, S02 p.6, S04 p.1, S04 p.3, S04 p.4.
