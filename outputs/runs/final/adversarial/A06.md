# A06 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Outside the supported scope. No AFS material shows optical fiber detection of trace metals in water.

**Requirements stated.** Trace lead at ppb level; Municipal drinking water; Continuous measurement; Fiber-optic probe installed in the distribution pipe.

**Key unknowns.** Lead form (dissolved, complexed, particulate) and alarm level; Whether an indicator or reagent is acceptable; Reference method, its uncertainty and applicable standard; Pipe conditions, fouling, distance to analyzer.

3. How do turbidity, particles, temperature, flow and water chemistry vary at the measuring point?
4. Is any indicator or reagent acceptable, and what response time, accuracy, standard and cleaning apply?
5. What problem should continuous data solve, and where would probe and analyzer sit?

### Recommendation

- **Premise unverifiable:** A fiber-optic probe in the distribution pipe can measure trace lead at ppb level continuously [S01 p.16; S01 p.5].
- **UV/Vis absorption (comparison in lab screening) (Transmission or transflection probe).** Transmission probes are described as ideal for transparent, low-OD bioprocess media; this is not established for lead in drinking water [S01 p.16]. Needs a defined lead-specific absorber; overlapping signals and low selectivity are expected [S01 p.5].
- **Fluorescence with a lead-selective indicator (hypothesis only) (Fluorimetric probe).** No AFS application example exists for lead; fluorimetry is only the most sensitive candidate if a justified indicator exists [S01 p.5]. High sensitivity does not imply selectivity; whether an indicator is permissible in drinking water is open [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

**Measurement concept.** Only if lab screening finds a lead-related signal: a transmission/transflection or fluorimetric probe for clear liquid [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Distribution pipe, as requested by the client; exact position not yet defined. No fiber link stated. UV-Vis silica fibers transmit about 190-1200 nm; the actual link and its coverage must be confirmed for the configuration [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: FLUO: UV-Vis silica covers the nominal range; UVVIS: UV-Vis silica covers the nominal range [S02 p.6; S01 p.12; S01 p.5]. Needs AFS confirmation: Existence of lead signal, Probe type and installation, Fiber link and length.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]; Fluorescence: UV/Vis excitation and emission; depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- No AFS application covers trace metals or drinking water. *(assumption)*
- Calibration is matrix-specific, so changes in sample matrix may require recalibration [DOCX §5].
- Turbidity or particles can mimic signal; particulate lead may be missed. *(general knowledge)*
- Neither method nor any indicator is guaranteed to reach ppb sensitivity. *(assumption)*
- Detection limit, accuracy, update rate and interface ratings are all unconfirmed [DOCX §3].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen spiked and real water samples offline with UV/Vis and fluorescence, with and without candidate indicators, to see whether any lead signal exists.
2. Record spectra with independent reference lead results and time-aligned sampling information.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on independent water samples or sampling campaigns.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements before any in-pipe trial.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria before testing; proceed only if the simplest configuration meets them on independent samples.
