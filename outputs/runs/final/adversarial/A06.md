# A06 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Outside the supported scope. No AFS material shows optical detection of lead at ppb in water.

**Requirements stated.** Trace lead at ppb level; Municipal drinking water; Continuous measurement; Fiber-optic probe installed in the distribution pipe.

**Key unknowns.** Lead form and alarm level; Reference method and uncertainty; Water clarity, flow, fouling; Response time, installation, applicable standard.

### Clarification questions

1. In which form is lead present (dissolved, complexed, particulate), at which alarm level, with which co-substances?
2. Which reference lab method, uncertainty and sampling timing provide lead values today?
3. How do turbidity, colour, particles, bubbles, temperature, flow and biofilm vary at the pipe?
4. What response time, accuracy, installation, cleaning and drinking-water standard must the system meet?
5. Which decision should the data drive, and are reagent-based or sampled measurements acceptable?

### Recommendation

- **Premise unverifiable:** A fiber-optic probe in the distribution pipe can measure trace lead at ppb level continuously [S01 p.16; S01 p.5].
- **UV/Vis spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** For highly transparent, low-optical-density liquids, UV transmission or transflection probes support accurate quantification of low-concentration analytes [S01 p.16]. UV spectra overlap strongly and selectivity is rated low, so chemometrics is needed; a specific absorbing species must be confirmed experimentally [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Fluorimetry (fluorimetric probe).** Fluorimetry is a highly sensitive emission method, a reason to screen it only if a justified fluorescent indicator exists [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. It is limited to samples containing a fluorescent component and has low selectivity [S04 p.4].

**Measurement concept.** Only if lab screening succeeds: a UV/Vis transmission or transflection probe for clear liquid [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Client wants the probe in the distribution pipe; feasibility of in-pipe installation is unassessed. No link stated; silica fibers cover UV/Vis and NIR [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type, in-pipe installation, optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]; Fluorimetry: UV and visible excitation and emission; depends on the species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- No AFS example supports optical lead detection at ppb. *(assumption)*
- A change in water matrix composition can invalidate a chemometric calibration. *(general knowledge)*
- Turbidity, particles or bubbles can mimic or mask a trace signal. *(general knowledge)*
- Neither candidate nor any combination is guaranteed to reach ppb. *(assumption)*
- Detection limit, update rate, interface ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen spiked and real water samples across client levels with UV/Vis and fluorescence to find any lead-related signal [S02 p.20].
2. Record spectra with independent reference lead results and time-aligned sampling information.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on independent water samples or sampling dates.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; proceed only if the simplest configuration meets them on independent samples.
