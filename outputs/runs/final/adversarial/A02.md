**Decision status.** Insufficient information. Cause of the off taste is unknown; no target can be defined yet.

**Requirements stated.** Juice bottling line sometimes fills off-tasting batches; Cause unknown; Wants UV/Vis on the filler to reject bad batches automatically; Told UV/Vis is extremely sensitive.

**Key unknowns.** Cause and level of the off taste; Reference method and its reliability; Juice optics and batch variation; Response time, installation, cleaning.

### Clarification questions

1. What might cause the off taste (spoilage, oxidation, contamination, raw material), and in which juices?
2. How is off taste judged today, how reliably, and are retained samples available?
3. Is the juice clear or cloudy, and how do colour, recipe and temperature vary?
4. Reject or alert; what response time, filler installation point and cleaning regime apply?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** UV/Vis is suitable for detecting off-taste batches because it is extremely sensitive [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5].
- **Premise unverifiable:** The off taste has a measurable optical signal that allows automatic rejection.
- **UV/Vis spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** Simplest low-cost candidate if a defined absorber is found; bioprocess example: 'For highly transparent liquids with low optical density, UV-Transmission or Transflection probes are ideal' [S01 p.16]. Bioprocess, not juice; low selectivity, so a link to off taste must first be shown in lab screening [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** Comparison candidate from a dairy example: 'NIR Transmission is the preferred method for transparent or semi-transparent liquids, such as skim milk, whey, or permeate.' [S01 p.18]. NIR may lack sensitivity for trace compounds below 0.1 percent [S01 p.16].

**Measurement concept.** If a target is found, UV/Vis or NIR transmission or transflection probes are the table's interfaces for liquids [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Client named the filler; a suitable juice-contact point is unconfirmed. No link stated; UV-Vis silica covers UV/Vis, Vis-NIR silica (350-2400 nm) covers the 0.78-2.4 um NIR band [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: UVVIS: UV-Vis silica covers the nominal range; NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um) [S02 p.6; S01 p.5; S01 p.10]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type, installation point on filler, cleaning compatibility.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]; NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Pulp, colour, turbidity or temperature can mimic an off-taste signal. *(general knowledge)*
- Recipe or juice-type changes can invalidate a model. *(general knowledge)*
- No method or combination is guaranteed to detect the off taste. *(assumption)*
- No juice application exists in the materials. *(assumption)*
- Detection ability, update rate and interface ratings are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen retained good and off batches offline with several methods for any spectral difference [S02 p.3].
2. Record spectra per candidate with time-aligned sensory or lab reference results.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out independent batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent batches.
