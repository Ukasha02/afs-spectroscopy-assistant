3. How do particle size, packing, temperature and composition differ between wheat, rye and oat batches?
4. What accuracy and response time are required, and how are the flours presented to the analyzer?
5. Do the supplier or your lab already hold rye or oat predictions compared with reference results?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** The wheat flour NIR moisture model will work on rye and oat flour without recalibration [S01 p.10; S02 p.12].
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** Water absorbs the same way in every flour [S01 p.10; S01 p.4].
- **Premise unverifiable:** The model can be used on the new flours next week.
- **NIR spectroscopy (existing instrument), flour-specific validation or recalibration (diffuse reflection).** The AFS page shows NIR fiber sensing for moisture (wood demo) and NIR spectra of flour, so NIR can be considered here but must be verified: "IoT Concept of NIR - LED Fiber Sensor to control Moisture Level" [S02 p.12]. We propose first testing the wheat model on rye and oat flour against reference results; NIR relies on chemometrics, so recalibration may be needed [S01 p.10].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe, listed for scattering media and powders (0.5-1 mm depth); surface at a distance is listed for solids and powders (2-5 mm) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Same presentation as for wheat, assumed until the client describes it. *(assumption)* No fiber link stated; silica fibers suit UV/Vis, NIR and Raman, while MIR needs IR fibers [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type, sample presentation, optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change from wheat to rye or oat matrix may affect the chemometric calibration NIR relies on [S01 p.10].
- Spectra carry particle-size information; size or packing differences may mimic moisture (hypothesis to test) [S01 p.4].
- Recalibration or flour-specific models are not guaranteed to meet requirements. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate and interface compatibility for new flours are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- The AFS moisture page is a concept demo on wood, not validated on flour [S02 p.12].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect rye and oat samples across the client-agreed moisture range, covering batch, particle-size and temperature variation.
2. Record NIR spectra with time-aligned independent reference moisture results for each sample.
3. Test the wheat model first; if needed, build flour-specific or global models and validate them on held-out batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and the ability to follow moisture changes against the client's requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria per flour before the test; use the simplest model that meets them on independent batches.
