# A01 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. Chalcogenide IR fiber cannot carry UV/Vis light; link must change.

**Requirements stated.** Coloured by-product followed in a fermenter; UV/Vis absorbance used today; Spectrometer to move to the control room; Leftover chalcogenide IR fiber (old FTIR project) as link.

**Key unknowns.** By-product identity, band, level; Broth turbidity, cell content; Distance, probe type, sterilization; Reference method, accuracy, response time.

### Clarification questions

1. Which by-product, at what level, absorbing at which wavelengths?
2. Which reference lab method, uncertainty and sampling timing quantify it?
3. How do turbidity, cell density and broth colour vary within and between runs?
4. What distance, probe port, sterilization, accuracy and response time apply?
5. What decision should control-room data drive?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** The leftover chalcogenide IR fiber can link the probe to the UV/Vis spectrometer [S02 p.6; S01 p.5].
- **Premise unverifiable:** The spectrometer can sit in the control room over a fiber link of the required length [S02 p.7].
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** UV/Vis absorbance adequately follows the coloured by-product in the broth [S01 p.5].
- **UV/Vis absorbance over UV-Vis silica fiber (transmission or transflection probe).** The bioprocess example supports this for clear media: "For highly transparent liquids with low optical density, UV-Transmission or Transflection probes are ideal" [S01 p.16]. Applies only if broth is clear; link must be UV-transmitting silica fiber [S01 p.16; S02 p.6].
- **UV/Vis absorbance over UV-Vis silica fiber (UV-ATR probe).** For turbid broth: "In these high -OD scenarios, a UV-ATR (Attenuated Total Reflection) probe is used." [S01 p.16]. The UV/Vis ATR probe is rated low sensitivity and low selectivity [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

**Measurement concept.** UV/Vis transmission or transflection probe if broth is clear; UV-ATR probe if turbid [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Probe in the fermenter; spectrometer in the control room, as requested. Rule check of the stated fiber: UVVIS: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR) none [S02 p.6; S01 p.5]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and geometry, Fiber length, UV loss, Sterilizable installation.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis absorbance: UV 190-360 nm and visible; depends on target and setup nominal [S01 p.5]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Chalcogenide fiber cannot carry UV/Vis; link length unknown [S02 p.6].
- Changing broth composition and overlapping UV signals can invalidate a calibration [S01 p.5].
- Cell turbidity can saturate a standard transmission setup [S01 p.16].
- Relocation may not preserve current signal quality. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, probe ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect broth samples across the client-agreed by-product range, run phases and turbidity variation.
2. Record UV/Vis spectra via transmission and UV-ATR over silica fiber, with time-aligned reference lab results.
3. Build single-method models per geometry; validate on held-out fermentation runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; choose the simplest probe and fiber link meeting them on independent runs.

**Sources.** S01 Table 1 (p.6), S01 p.16, S01 p.5, S02 p.6, S02 p.7.
