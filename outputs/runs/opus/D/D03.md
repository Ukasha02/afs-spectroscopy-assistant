### Recommendation

- **Premise: Spectroscopy can track protein concentration in the whey stream as it changes.** — plausible to be tested. The dairy example names NIR transmission for whey, but whey protein tracking is not shown, so this must be tested [S01 p.18].
- **Premise: The stream remains relatively clear, so a clear-liquid concept may apply.** — unverifiable. The client reports turbid states, and transmission can fail when the liquid becomes too thick; the turbidity extent is unknown [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission / transflection probe).** The dairy example names whey: "NIR Transmission is the preferred method for transparent or semi-transparent liquids, such as skim milk, whey, or permeate." [S01 p.18]. Pathlength must be controlled, usually 1-10 mm, so signal is neither absorbed nor from negligible volume; verify for clear and turbid states [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection probe).** Reflection suits scattering dairy products: "Inline NIR Reflection is primarily used for opaque or highly concentrated dairy products, such as yogurt, butter, or whole milk concentrates." [S01 p.17]. Test only if turbid states defeat transmission; reflection can be less representative for clear liquids [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** NIR transmission/transflection probe; diffuse reflection probe as comparison [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Whey line before the concentration stage; exact point unknown. ### Risks and uncertainty

- Whey type or composition changes can invalidate calibration [DOCX §5].
- Turbidity changes can mimic or mask protein changes [S01 p.17; S01 p.18].
- Neither geometry is guaranteed to handle all turbidity. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Protein fouling may drift the signal. *(general knowledge)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect whey samples across the client-agreed protein range, covering clear and turbid operating states and temperatures.
2. Record transflection and reflection spectra with time-aligned laboratory protein references.
3. Build single-geometry models first; validate on held-out runs that include turbid conditions.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and the ability to follow changes against the client's requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest geometry meeting them on independent runs.
