### Recommendation

- **Premise: An optical measurement can follow yeast growth and reveal changes earlier.** — plausible to be tested. S01 reports that combining NIR and fluorescence demonstrated the possibility of more accurate biomass determination in yeast fermentation. This is a reason to test, not proof for this process [S01 p.7]. Earlier detection of changes depends on the undefined metabolic target and has not been shown for this process. *(assumption)*
- **Premise: The broth's increasing optical density is a usable indicator of growth.** — plausible to be tested. Cell suspension turbidity correlates with cell density, but optical density is not a viable cell count [S01 p.16; DOCX §5]. Other broth components may also contribute to optical density. This must be checked against an independent biomass reference. *(assumption)*
- **UV/Vis spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe; UV-ATR if signal saturates).** Turbidity is cited as a low-cost biomass trend in cell suspensions [S01 p.16]. Transmission can saturate in dense broth; UV-ATR is cited for high optical density [S01 p.16].
- **Fluorescence spectroscopy (fluorimetric probe).** Fluorescence is cited for bioprocess detection of cofactors such as NADH, a possible metabolic signal [S01 p.16]. Low selectivity; applies only to an agreed fluorescent species [S01 Table 1 (p.6); DOCX §5].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** NIR is cited for major components such as glucose; yeast biomass only with NIR plus fluorescence [S01 p.16; S01 p.7]. May lack trace sensitivity; calibration is matrix-specific [S01 p.16; DOCX §5].
- **NIR + fluorescence (two probes or a combi probe, time-aligned).** A demonstrated possibility of more accurate yeast biomass determination is reported for this pair [S01 p.7]. Only after each method is assessed alone; any gain is a hypothesis to test [S01 p.8; DOCX §5].

### Risks and uncertainty

- Strain or medium changes can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Bubbles, foam and turbidity can mimic biomass changes. *(general knowledge)*
- Optical density tracks cell density, not viable cells [S01 p.16; DOCX §5].
- No method or combination is guaranteed to help [S01 p.7; DOCX §5].
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen samples from several run stages with UV/Vis, fluorescence and NIR to define targets, spectral range and concentration level.
2. Record spectra with time-aligned independent biomass and metabolite references.
3. Build single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
