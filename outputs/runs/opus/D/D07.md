### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** An optical measurement can detect API composition changes while the melt is processed [S02 p.14; DOCX §3].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection probe at the die).** The AFS extrusion slide shows NIR reflection in-line: "NIR - Reflex & MIR - ATR - spectroscopy to control polymer extrusion in - line" [S02 p.14]. Overlapping NIR bands suggest chemometric analysis; any calibration must be built and tested on representative melt samples [S01 p.10].
- **MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy (diamond ATR probe with PIR fiber).** The same slide shows: "Spectra of API in hot polymer obtained with Diamond cone ATR probe coupled to iC10 FTIR (MTAC)" [S02 p.14]. ATR probes only 1-6 um deep, so contact, fouling and compatibility matter [S01 Table 1 (p.6); DOCX §5].

**Measurement concept.** Compare an NIR diffuse reflection probe with an MIR ATR probe on representative melt [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S02 p.14]. Near the die; exact point and contact allowance from the client [S02 p.14]. No link stated; NIR on silica, MIR-ATR on PIR fiber, to confirm [S02 p.6; S02 p.14]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um); MIR: Vis-NIR silica no coverage; polycrystalline IR (PIR) partial (3-17 um) [S02 p.6; S01 p.10; S01 p.9]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and tip material, Temperature and pressure rating, Installation port and cleaning.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]; MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy: 4000–400 cm-1 nominal [S01 p.9]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change of API, polymer or formulation can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Melt temperature, bubbles or inhomogeneity may mimic composition changes; to be tested. *(assumption)*
- Tip build-up may drift or mask the signal.
- No method or combination is guaranteed to track API changes [DOCX §3].
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed [DOCX §3].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Prepare melts across the client-agreed API range with temperature, bubble and homogeneity variation.
2. Record NIR and MIR-ATR spectra with time-aligned offline assays.
3. Build single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias, ability to follow changes and fouling against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
