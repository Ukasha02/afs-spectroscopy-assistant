### Recommendation

- **Premise plausible to be tested:** An in situ spectroscopy pilot can follow glucose and lactate and be checked against existing laboratory measurements [S01 p.16].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman fiber probe, in situ).** The bioprocess example uses Raman for glucose and lactate in aqueous media [S01 p.16]. Check fluorescence background from the medium first [S01 p.11].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe).** Comparison candidate; the bioprocess example uses NIR for major components such as glucose [S01 p.16]. NIR may lack sensitivity at trace levels [S01 p.16].

**Measurement concept.** Raman probe and NIR transmission/transflection probe for liquids [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. ### Risks and uncertainty

- Medium changes can invalidate a matrix-specific calibration [DOCX §5].
- Cell density and turbidity change the signal and can saturate transmission [S01 p.16].
- No method or combination is guaranteed to help. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, sterilization ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Probe fouling by cells or proteins may degrade signal. *(general knowledge)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Sample across client-agreed glucose and lactate ranges, medium, cell density and temperature.
2. Record Raman and NIR spectra with time-aligned lab references.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
