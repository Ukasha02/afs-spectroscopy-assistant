**Key unknowns.** Fat range and grade classes; Reference method and uncertainty; Contact or stand-off, conveyor speed; Accuracy, response time, cleaning.

### Recommendation

- **Premise: The combined probe shown on chicken tissue will grade carcasses by fat content.** — plausible to be tested. In the lab chicken study, IR worked better than fluorescence for discriminating fat, but did not fully separate the three tissue classes on its own. This is tissue classification, not fat-content grading [S04 p.4]. A published application is a reason to test, not proof for a new customer [DOCX §3].
- **Premise: The method is already validated and can be presented to customers as validated.** — contradicted. The probe was demonstrated only on model tissue samples in the laboratory, with no industrial or grading validation [S04 p.1].
- **Premise: The probe can be mounted over the conveyor and grade every carcass.** — contradicted. The paper states that ATR measurement requires samples allowing tight contact with the probe head. The ATR probe rows list liquid, paste and soft samples at 1-6 um optical depth [S04 p.4; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection, surface at a distance).** The dairy example uses a flush-mounted reflection probe for opaque products; at-a-distance diffuse reflectance is a general NIR capability [S01 p.17; S01 p.10]. Dairy is another matrix and reflection is surface-sensitive; a meat calibration must be built and tested [S01 p.17].
- **MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy (ATR probe, lab comparison on cut samples).** In a lab chicken study, IR discriminated fat via the ester carbonyl band, but alone did not fully separate all classes [S04 p.4; S04 p.3; S04 p.4]. ATR needs tight contact and probes 1–6 µm deep; use it for lab comparison only [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.4].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection at a distance; MIR-ATR probe on cut lab samples [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Over the conveyor, as the client requested. No link stated. Approximate ranges: Vis-NIR silica 350–2400 nm; CIR 1.1–6.5 µm, PIR 3–17 µm; coverage needs checking [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR full range: Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; partial: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG); MIR full range: no single fiber; partial: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Polycrystalline silver halide IR fiber (PIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Stand-off reflection probe type, Installation and hygiene rating, Optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal; usable band set by fiber and instrument); MIR (FTIR) spectroscopy: 4000–400 cm-1 nominal [S01 p.9]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Model-tissue lab results cannot back a customer validation claim [S04 p.1].
- Breed, cut or feed changes can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Surface condition can mimic fat changes in reflection [S01 p.17].
- Neither NIR nor a combination is guaranteed to grade adequately. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, speed, hygiene ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect carcasses across the agreed fat range and surface, temperature and size variation.
2. Record NIR and lab MIR-ATR spectra with time-matched independent reference fat results.
3. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out batches; combine only if justified.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against grading requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client first; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent carcasses.
