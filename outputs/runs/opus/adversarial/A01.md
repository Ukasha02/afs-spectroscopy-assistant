# A01 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. Chalcogenide IR fiber does not transmit the UV/Vis range.

**Requirements stated.** Coloured by-product followed in fermenter by UV/Vis absorbance; Spectrometer to move to control room; Reuse leftover chalcogenide IR fiber from FTIR project as probe link.

**Key unknowns.** By-product band and level; Broth turbidity variation; Control-room distance; reference method; Conditions, cleaning, accuracy needs.

### Clarification questions

1. Which by-product, absorbance band and concentration range (ppm or %) must be followed?
2. Which reference method, uncertainty and sampling time provide by-product values?
3. How do turbidity, cells and bubbles vary within and between batches?
4. What accuracy, response time, sterilization and cleaning conditions apply?
5. How far is the control room?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: The leftover chalcogenide IR fiber can link the fermenter probe to the UV/Vis spectrometer.** — contradicted. Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber transmits about 1100-6500 nm, so it cannot carry the UV or visible light the method uses; UV-Vis silica fiber covers 190-1200 nm [S02 p.6]. UV/Vis spectroscopy works in the UV region (190-360 nm) and visible, and needs special fiber optic probes [S01 p.5].
- **Premise: UV/Vis absorbance can follow the by-product remotely from the control room.** — plausible to be tested. Fiber probes support remote in-situ measurement, and UV/Vis is used in bioprocess monitoring, but the materials show it for biomass, not for this by-product [S01 p.5; S01 p.16]. Link length and UV attenuation over the client's distance are unknown. *(assumption)*
- **UV/Vis absorbance (Transmission or transflection probe with UV-Vis silica fiber).** Bioprocess example: "For highly transparent liquids with low optical density, UV-Transmission or Transflection probes are ideal" [S01 p.16]. Low selectivity; by-product specificity needs testing [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5].
- **UV/Vis absorbance (UV-ATR probe with UV-Vis silica fiber).** Same example: "In these high -OD scenarios, a UV-ATR (Attenuated Total Reflection) probe is used." [S01 p.16]. Only if transmission saturates; rated low sensitivity [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

**Measurement concept.** Transmission/transflection probe in the broth; UV-ATR if optical density is too high [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.16]. Probe in fermenter; spectrometer in control room. UV-Vis silica fiber instead of the chalcogenide cable; length needs confirmation [S02 p.6]. Rule check of the stated fiber: UVVIS: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR) none [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and path, UV fiber length, Sterilizable installation.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis absorbance: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Chalcogenide fiber cannot carry UV/Vis light [S02 p.6].
- Turbidity and high optical density alter or saturate the UV/Vis signal, confounding absorbance [S01 p.16].
- Medium or strain changes can invalidate a calibration. *(general knowledge)*
- Neither geometry is guaranteed to track the by-product. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, update rate, link length and probe ratings are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect broth samples across the agreed by-product range, batch stages and turbidity variation.
2. Record UV/Vis spectra per candidate geometry via UV-Vis silica fiber, with time-aligned reference values.
3. Build single-geometry models; validate on held-out batches.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent batches.

**Sources.** S01 Table 1 (p.6), S01 p.16, S01 p.5, S02 p.6.
