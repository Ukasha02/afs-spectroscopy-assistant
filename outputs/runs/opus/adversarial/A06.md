# A06 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Outside the supported scope. No AFS material shows optical fiber measurement of trace metals in water.

**Requirements stated.** Trace lead at ppb level; Municipal drinking water; Continuous measurement; Fiber-optic probe installed in the distribution pipe.

**Key unknowns.** Lead form and limit; Reference method and detection limit; Turbidity, fouling, flow; Response time, analyzer distance.

### Clarification questions

1. Which lead forms, concentration range and regulatory limit apply?
2. Which reference method, detection limit and sampling schedule exist?
3. How do turbidity, particles, temperature, flow and biofilm vary?
4. Which response time, alarm purpose, standards and installation access apply?
5. How far is the analyzer; is a bypass with reagents acceptable?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: A fiber-optic probe in the distribution pipe can continuously measure trace lead at ppb level.** — unverifiable. The materials give no application example for heavy metals or drinking water. *(assumption)* NIR may lack sensitivity for trace compounds below 0.1 percent, far above ppb [S01 p.16]. Fluorimetry needs a fluorescent species; most chemicals do not fluoresce [S01 p.5]. Lead at ppb is usually determined by elemental laboratory methods, not molecular spectroscopy. *(general knowledge)*
- **UV/Vis spectroscopy (transmission or transflection probe).** Lab screening candidate only, given its UV sensitivity [S01 p.5]. Low selectivity; other constituents could mimic or mask lead [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5].
- **Fluorescence spectroscopy (fluorimetric probe).** Lab screening only if a justified fluorescent lead indicator exists [S01 p.5]. Measures only a defined fluorescent species or justified indicator [DOCX §5; S01 p.5].
- **Not recommended: NIR, MIR, Raman (transmission, ATR, Raman probe).** NIR may lack sensitivity below 0.1 percent [S01 p.16]. Reconsider only if lab screening reveals a usable signal.

**Measurement concept.** If pursued: UV/Vis transmission or transflection probe; fluorimetric probe only with an indicator [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Distribution pipe, as stated. UV-Vis silica fiber covers both ranges; no link stated, length and route unknown [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: UVVIS full range: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated); partial: Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; FLUO full range: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated); partial: Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type for pipe, Installation and pressure rating, Optical path length.

**Spectral range.** UV/Vis: UV 190-360 nm and visible nominal [S01 p.5]; Fluorescence: Depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Cited AFS pages show no trace-metal water example. *(assumption)*
- Calibrations are matrix-specific; changed water composition may require recalibration [DOCX §5].
- Turbidity and particles can mask a weak signal [S01 p.16].
- Reaching ppb with any method or combination is an untested hypothesis. *(assumption)*
- Detection limit, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Screen representative and lead-spiked water by UV/Vis and fluorescence to check whether any lead signal exists.
2. Record spectra with independent reference lead results and timing information.
3. Build single-method models only if a signal exists; validate on independent water samples.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent samples, or stop.
