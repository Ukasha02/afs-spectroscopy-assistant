### Recommendation

- **Premise: Spectroscopy can track protein in the whey stream as it changes.** — plausible to be tested. Dairy example names NIR transmission for whey, but gives no protein-in-whey performance [S01 p.18].
- **Premise: Laboratory results are a suitable reference for in-line calibration.** — plausible to be tested. Reference accuracy and sampling time unknown; time-aligned samples needed. *(assumption)*
- **Premise: One geometry can handle both clear and turbid whey.** — plausible to be tested. Reflection suits scattering samples but is less representative for clear liquids [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe (1-10 mm path)).** Transmission suits clear whey, as the dairy example names it [S01 p.18]. Pathlength must be controlled: too wide loses signal, too narrow samples little volume [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Diffuse reflection probe).** Reflection is the comparison candidate for turbid states in the dairy example [S01 p.17]. Less representative for clear liquids; a hypothesis for turbid states only [S01 p.18].

**Measurement concept.** NIR transmission or transflection probe (1-10 mm path) for clear whey; transflection tested for turbid states [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.18]. In-line in the whey stream before the concentration stage; exact point unknown. VIS-NIR silica fiber covers 0.35-2.4 um; link length unknown [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR full range: Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; partial: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG) [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and path length, Installation point and link length, Turbidity-tolerant optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm (nominal method range) nominal [S01 p.10]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change in whey composition or matrix can invalidate the calibration [DOCX §5].
- Morphological factors such as process turbulence must be considered; they can complicate measurement [S01 p.7].
- Neither NIR alone nor a combination is guaranteed to track protein across turbidity [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, response time, interface ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Protein films on the probe window may change the signal; cleaning compatibility unknown. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect whey across the agreed protein range, clear and turbid states, and process variation.
2. Record spectra per geometry with time-aligned lab protein results and turbidity notes.
3. Build single-geometry NIR models first; validate on held-out runs before any combination.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and tracking of changes against agreed requirements, per geometry.

**Decision basis.** Agree accuracy, bias and response targets before testing; choose the simplest geometry meeting them on independent runs.
