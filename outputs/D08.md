**Requirements stated.** Lab classification study on chicken muscle, fat and liver model tissues; Compare MIR ATR and fluorescence information; Sequential measurements may miss the same location; Needs a first experiment and what its results would show.

**Key unknowns.** Target classes beyond the three tissues; Fluorescent species of interest, if any; Sample form, ATR contact, spot marking; Reference method, sample numbers, success criteria.

### Recommendation

- **Premise: Sequential measurements may not sample the same location, so same-spot measurement is needed.** — plausible to be tested. Alternating measurements cannot guarantee the same spot; a combined probe addresses this in a model-tissue study [S04 p.2].
- **Premise: The study will yield a clear statement of what its results demonstrate.** — unverifiable. Depends on reference method, sample numbers and success criteria, not yet defined. *(assumption)*
- **MIR ATR probe (ATR tip contacting the marked tissue surface).** ATR-IR separated fat best but not all three tissues alone; it supports one arm of a combined approach [S04 p.4]. ATR samples only about 1-6 µm deep, so tight contact and surface quality matter for the spectra [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.4].
- **Fluorescence fiber probe (Fluorescence probe at the same marked spot).** Fluorescence was well suited to recognising liver, with a 520–530 nm band mainly from FAD [S04 p.3; S04 p.4]. Fluorescence applies only to samples with at least one fluorescent component [S04 p.4].
- **MIR ATR + fluorescence combi probe (Combined ATR tip and fluorescence fibers at one spot).** Fused IR and fluorescence data separated the three tissues where single methods did not [S04 p.4]. Only after each method is assessed alone; the gain is a hypothesis to test [S01 p.7].

**Measurement concept.** Combi probe (ATR tip plus fluorescence fibers) or two probes with spot marking [S04 p.2; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Lab: ATR tip on the marked spot of each tissue piece. CIR fiber for MIR and UV-Vis silica for fluorescence, as in S04; ranges to be checked [S04 p.2; S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and ATR tip, Combi probe optical configuration, Fiber type and MIR range.

**Spectral range.** MIR (ATR): 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 µm) nominal [S01 p.9]; Fluorescence: UV/Vis excitation and emission; depends on fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12] (Excitation source not yet defined). Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Few-sample models are exploratory and may not transfer to new batches [S03 p.10].
- Sample position and tissue heterogeneity can cause strong spectral variability [S03 p.7].
- Fusion may not beat the best single method; the gain is a hypothesis [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, interface compatibility and instrument fit are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- CIR fiber reaches about 6.5 µm, not the full MIR range [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Prepare several pieces each of chicken muscle, fat and liver, with marked spots and expected handling variation [S04 p.3].
2. Record MIR-ATR and fluorescence spectra from the same marked spots, with independent reference labels and timing.
3. Build single-method models first; a fused model only if justified; validate on held-out pieces or batches [S03 p.14].
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes per method and fused, against agreed requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree error, bias and classification targets with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent pieces.
