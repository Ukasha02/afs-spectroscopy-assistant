# D08 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Provisional feasibility assessment. Lab comparison is testable; tissue classes, references and fluorescent species need definition.

**Requirements stated.** Model tissues: chicken muscle, fat and liver; lab classification study; Compare MIR ATR and fluorescence information; Heterogeneous material; sequential measurements may miss the same spot; Needs a first comparison experiment and what it would demonstrate.

**Key unknowns.** Tissue classes and reference labels; Fluorescent species present; any indicator added; Preparation, freshness, storage and replicates; Interface: ATR probe, combi probe or bench; fibers.

### Clarification questions

1. Which classes must be separated, and which fluorescent species or indicator is expected in each?
2. Which reference method gives the class labels, and how certain is each label?
3. How do cut surface, contact pressure, freshness and storage change spectra between runs?
4. What error level, timeline and contact conditions are acceptable?
5. Is a same-spot interface (combi probe, FTIR bench, fibers) available, and what changes if classes separate reliably?

### Recommendation

- **Premise: Fluorescence adds class information for these tissues (implicit in the comparison).** — plausible to be tested. Fluorescence was well suited to recognising liver tissue, but no single technique fully separated the three classes; it needs a fluorescent component [S04 p.4].
- **MIR (IR spectroscopy), ATR platform (ATR contact on sample surface, lab bench).** In the chicken model, IR spectroscopy discriminated fat better, supporting ATR-IR as the first arm [S04 p.4]. Only three samples per tissue type were used; ATR depth is about 1-6 um, so contact and spot need checking [S04 p.3; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Fluorimetry, fluorimetric probe (fluorescence probe, sample surface).** In the same model, fluorescence was well suited for the recognition of liver tissue, a comparison candidate for the second arm [S04 p.4]. Fluorescence needs a fluorescent species; the model covers only samples that contain one [S04 p.4].
- **MIR ATR + fluorescence, combi probe (same-spot ATR and fluorescence probe).** Fused IR and fluorescence data gave three distinct groups of chicken tissue types [S04 p.4]. Only after each method has been assessed on its own; a hypothesis to test, with same-spot measurement required [S04 p.2].

**Measurement concept.** MIR: ATR platform or ATR probe. Fluorescence: fluorimetric probe. Option: one combi ATR-fluorescence probe [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.1]. Sample surface in contact with the ATR crystal; same spot for both arms in the combi option [S01 p.8]. MIR arm needs mid-IR fiber (CIR or PIR); fluorescence arm uses silica. Link not defined [S04 p.2; S02 p.6]. Transmission per S02 p.6: CIR 1.1-6.5 um, PIR 3-17 um, UV-Vis silica 0.19-1.2 um. Full coverage of the MIR range (2.5-25 um) is an inference: none spans it fully [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: MIR full range: no single fiber; partial: Chalcogenide As-S IR fiber (CIR), Polycrystalline silver halide IR fiber (PIR), Hollow glass waveguide (HWG), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber; FLUO full range: UV-Vis silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated); partial: Vis-NIR silica fiber (standard or Al/Cu-coated), Indium fluoride fiber, Zirconium fluoride fiber [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: ATR probe type and tip, Combi probe availability, Fiber link and FTIR coupling.

**Spectral range.** MIR (ATR): 4000-400 cm-1 (2.5-25 um) nominal [S01 p.9]; Fluorescence: UV and visible regions; depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12] (Excitation set after lab screening). Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Tissue heterogeneity (e.g. fat) lowers measurement reproducibility [S04 p.3].
- A change of tissue type, preparation or matrix can invalidate a calibration. *(assumption)*
- Neither a method nor the combination is guaranteed to improve classification [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Chicken study: lab model, three samples per tissue type; not proof for other tissues [S04 p.3].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Prepare chicken muscle, fat and liver in replicates with a fixed protocol, storage time and cut-surface variation.
2. Measure MIR ATR and fluorescence alone and combined at the same spot, with independent reference labels and timing information per location.
3. Build single-method models first; a combined model only if justified, validated on held-out batches or independent samples.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and the ability to follow changes against the group's agreed requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numeric error and bias criteria with the group before testing; choose the simplest configuration that meets them on independent material.
