### Recommendation

- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission/transflection or diffuse reflection, chosen by broth optics).** The bioprocess example states: "NIR spectroscopy is effective for tracking major components like glucose or glycerol" [S01 p.16]. NIR may lack sensitivity below 0.1 percent; glucose levels must be checked [S01 p.16].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman probe).** The same example supports Raman for "specific metabolites like glucose, acetate, or lactate" [S01 p.16]. Fluorescence of many organic compounds can overlap and exceed the Raman signal [S01 p.11].
- **Fluorescence (existing spectrometer) (fluorimetric probe).** Fluorescence can detect cofactors such as "NADH, tryptophan, and riboflavin" [S01 p.16]. Usable only as an indirect indicator once a reference relationship to viable biomass is established [DOCX §5].
- **NIR + fluorescence (separate or combined probes at one location).** For yeast fermentation, NIR with fluorescence "demonstrated the possibility of more accurately determining the biomass content" [S01 p.7]. Only after each method is assessed alone; a hypothesis to test [S01 p.7].

### Risks and uncertainty

- Medium, strain or process changes can invalidate a calibration [DOCX §5].
- Turbid broth can saturate transmission signals; probe choice, e.g. UV-ATR, follows optical density [S01 p.16].
- Optical density is not a viable cell count [DOCX §5].
- No method or combination is guaranteed to help [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, update rate, ratings and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect broth samples from several runs across agreed glucose, biomass and turbidity ranges.
2. Record NIR, Raman and fluorescence spectra with time-aligned glucose and viable-cell references.
3. Single-method models first, combined only if justified; validate on held-out runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
