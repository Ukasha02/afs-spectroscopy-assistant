### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** Fluorescence can directly report glucose concentration during fermentation [S01 p.5; S01 p.16].
- **Premise unverifiable:** Fluorescence alone can directly report viable biomass [S01 p.7].
- **Premise contradicted:** Fluorescence sensitivity alone justifies the method [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** The existing fluorescence spectrometer can be reused for this fermentation [S01 p.16].
- **NIR spectroscopy (transmission/transflection or diffuse reflection probe).** The bioprocess example supports NIR for glucose: "NIR spectroscopy is effective for tracking major components like glucose or glycerol" [S01 p.16]. Overlapping bands suggest chemometrics; a calibration for this medium must be built and tested [S01 p.10].
- **Raman spectroscopy (Raman probe).** The same example names Raman for "specific metabolites like glucose, acetate, or lactate" [S01 p.16]. Fluorescence background may swamp the Raman signal; check signal quality first [S01 p.11].
- **NIR + existing fluorescence (separate or combined probes, time-aligned).** Supported for fermentation biomass: "more accurately determining the biomass content during yeast fermentation" [S01 p.7]. Only after each method is assessed alone; any gain and any viability link are hypotheses to test [S01 p.7].

**Measurement concept.** Lab screening: NIR transmission/transflection or diffuse reflection probe, Raman probe, existing fluorimetric probe [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. In the fermenter at an agreed point, only after successful lab screening. No fiber link stated; silica fibers suit UV/Vis, NIR and Raman, while MIR needs IR fibers [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and geometry, Sterilisable installation, Existing spectrometer compatibility.

**Spectral range.** NIR: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]; Raman: Raman shift (cm-1); recorded at 400-1100 nm nominal [S01 p.11] (Excitation not chosen; state separately); Fluorescence: UV/Vis; depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- A change of medium or organism can invalidate a calibration. *(general knowledge)*
- Turbidity follows cell density, so optical signals change with growth; mimicking composition is untested [S01 p.16].
- Neither a single method nor a combination is guaranteed to help [S01 p.7].
- Accuracy, update rate and spectrometer compatibility are all unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- No fiber link stated; its compatibility with each method needs confirmation. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect broth samples across the agreed glucose range, growth phases, turbidity and medium variation.
2. Record NIR, Raman and fluorescence spectra with time-aligned independent glucose and viability references.
3. Single-method models first, combined only if justified; validate on held-out fermentation runs.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and the ability to follow changes against the client's requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria before the test; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent runs.
