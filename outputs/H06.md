**Decision status.** Premise not supported / concept not approved. In-vitro proof-of-concept study cannot demonstrate readiness for live-surgery boundary decisions.

**Requirements stated.** Read the LED NIR kidney study; Wants same concept for live-surgery tumor boundaries; No new in vivo data; No validation on own setup.

**Key unknowns.** Tumor type and surgical decision; Histopathology reference, spot co-registration; Tissue and patient variation; Accuracy, response time, sterilisation, regulatory route.

### Clarification questions

1. Which tumor and healthy tissue must be distinguished, for which surgical decision?
2. Which histopathology reference confirms each spot, and how are spots co-registered?
3. How do blood, fat, surface state and patient factors vary?
4. What accuracy, response time, sterilisation and regulatory route apply?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** The kidney study findings can be quoted as proof that the proposed sensor is ready for live-surgery boundary decisions [S03 p.14; S03 p.10].
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** The LED NIR kidney concept transfers directly to intraoperative tumor boundary guidance [S03 p.3].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection fiber probe (full spectrum first, then few LED wavelengths)).** The in-vitro kidney study supports testing NIR diffuse reflectance for tumor discrimination, not readiness [S03 p.14]. The LED set should be adapted to each tissue; full-spectrum data allow sensor simulation first [S03 p.14; S03 p.10].
- **Fluorescence spectroscopy (fluorescence fiber probe).** The tumor-margin example lists a fluorescence fiber probe as a candidate [S01 p.13]. Needs a fluorescent tissue component; the source table rates fluorimetry selectivity low [S04 p.4; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **NIR + fluorescence (same-spot measurement, e.g. combi probe).** Materials report complementary NIR and fluorescence information for malignant versus benign tissue [S01 p.14]. Only after each method is assessed alone; the gain is a hypothesis to test at the same spot [S01 p.8; S04 p.2].

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe; fluorescence probe as comparison [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.13]. Ex vivo tissue with histopathology first; intraoperative use only after validation and approval. No link stated; Vis-NIR silica transmits 350–2400 nm; fluorescence coverage depends on the fluorophore wavelengths [S02 p.6]. No fiber link stated. Rule check: NIR: Vis-NIR silica partial (0.78-2.4 um); FLUO: UV-Vis silica covers the nominal range [S02 p.6; S01 p.10; S01 p.12]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type and geometry, sterilisable probe design, optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10]; Fluorescence spectroscopy: UV/Vis; depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- In-vitro results from few patients do not establish in vivo use [S03 p.3; DOCX §3].
- Models may fail on other tumor types or patients [S03 p.10].
- Position and biopsy differences may cause variability confusable with tissue differences [S03 p.7].
- No method or combination is guaranteed to help [DOCX §5].
- Accuracy, response time, sterilisation and compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect paired ex vivo tumor and normal tissue across agreed tumor types, patients and optical variation.
2. Record NIR and fluorescence spectra at co-registered spots with independent histopathology reference and timing information.
3. Single-method models first, combined only if justified; validate on held-out patients [S03 p.13].
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow boundary changes against agreed requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree numerical acceptance criteria before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent patients.
