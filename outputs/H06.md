2. Which reference confirms boundaries, with what uncertainty, matched to the probe spot?
3. How do blood, fat, motion and tissue type vary between patients?
4. What accuracy, false-negative tolerance, response time, sterilization and regulatory path apply?

### Recommendation

- **Premise contradicted:** The kidney study findings can be quoted as proof that the proposed sensor is ready for tumor boundary guidance in live surgery [S03 p.1; S03 p.3; S03 p.10].
- **Premise plausible to be tested:** The LED NIR concept can guide tumor boundary decisions during live surgery [S03 p.14].
- **NIR spectroscopy (diffuse reflection probe).** The supplied kidney study tested an LED NIR diffuse-reflectance sensor on ex vivo tissue [S03 p.2]. LED wavelengths should be adapted to each tissue and cancer type; false negatives are unacceptable [S03 p.14].
- **NIR + fluorescence (same-spot NIR reflection + fluorescence probes).** Fluorimetry plus diffuse-reflectance NIR improved tumor margin detection in preliminary tissue studies [S04 p.1]. Only after each method is assessed alone; the gain is a hypothesis to test.

**Measurement concept.** NIR diffuse reflection probe for scattering tissue [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Ex vivo paired tissue in the lab first; intraoperative use only after validation and approvals. No fiber link stated; silica fibers suit UV/Vis, NIR and Raman, while MIR needs IR fibers [S02 p.6]. Needs AFS confirmation: Probe type and geometry, Sterilizable or single-use probe, Optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR: 780–2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal; select LEDs from own spectra); Fluorescence: Depends on the fluorescent species nominal [S01 p.12]. Usable range depends on the selected instrument and fiber; no optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Few-patient ex vivo results do not show clinical readiness [S03 p.10].
- Models depend on cancer type, grade and staging; other tissues need adaptation [S03 p.10; S03 p.14].
- Sample size and probe position may have caused spectral variability [S03 p.7].
- No method or combination is guaranteed to work in vivo. *(assumption)*
- Accuracy, response time, sterilization, compatibility unconfirmed. *(assumption)*

### First feasibility experiment

1. Collect ethically approved paired ex vivo tumor and normal tissue across patients, grades and tissue types.
2. Record NIR, LED and optional fluorescence data with independent histopathology reference at marked spots, with timing information.
3. Single-method models first, fusion only if justified; validate on held-out patients.
4. Compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow boundary changes against client requirements.

**Decision basis.** Agree acceptance criteria, including false-negative tolerance, beforehand; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent patients.
