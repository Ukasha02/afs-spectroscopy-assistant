# D03 · First technical assessment

**Decision status.** Provisional feasibility assessment. Protein range, turbidity and reference accuracy are unknown; first concept only.

**Requirements stated.** Relatively clear whey stream upstream of a concentration stage; Check whether spectroscopy tracks protein, with lab results as reference; Stream can become more turbid under some conditions; first concept must handle this.

**Key unknowns.** Protein level and range (ppm or %); Turbidity range and cause; Lab reference method, accuracy, sampling time; Temperature, cleaning, installation, analyzer distance, fibers, response time.

### Recommendation

- **Premise: Spectroscopy can track protein concentration in the whey stream as the stream changes.** — plausible to be tested. The dairy example uses NIR with PLS for milk fat and protein; whey protein is not shown [S01 p.17].
- **Premise: Laboratory results are an adequate reference for the measurement.** — unverifiable. Reference method, accuracy and sampling time are not yet documented. *(assumption)*
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission or transflection probe in the clear whey stream).** The dairy example names NIR transmission preferred for clear or semi-transparent liquids such as whey [S01 p.18]. Light cannot penetrate when milk is too thick or fat-rich; turbid states need separate checks [S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Diffuse reflection probe, flush-mounted, for turbid states).** Reflection is named for opaque or concentrated dairy where transmission fails [S01 p.17; S01 p.18]. Sensitive to surface and fat globule size; less representative for clear liquids [S01 p.17; S01 p.18].
- **NIR spectroscopy (Transmission plus diffuse reflection, combined or dual probe).** Combining methods can give synergy where one method alone is insufficient [S01 p.7]. A gain is a hypothesis to test, after each method is assessed alone [DOCX §5].

**Measurement concept.** NIR transmission or transflection probe in the clear whey stream, upstream of the concentration stage [S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. Diffuse reflection probe, flush-mounted in the pipe, as candidate for turbid states [S01 p.17]. Vis-NIR silica covers about 0.35–2.4 µm; the 780–2500 nm NIR range is only partly covered; link length not stated [S02 p.6; S01 p.10]. Needs AFS confirmation: probe type and path length, installation point and access, turbid-state optical configuration.

**Spectral range.** NIR spectroscopy: 780-2500 nm nominal [S01 p.10] (Nominal method range; instrument and fiber coverage must be checked). Specify only the usable range of the selected instrument and fiber; do not pick an optimal band without sample spectra.

### Risks and uncertainty

- Calibration is matrix-specific, so transfer to other stream states is not assumed [DOCX §5].
- Scattering from fat globules and surface effects is physical interference in the reflectance signal [S01 p.17].
- Neither a single method nor a combination is guaranteed to work across clear and turbid states. *(general knowledge)*
- Accuracy, update rate, interface ratings and equipment compatibility are unconfirmed. *(assumption)*
- Link length and existing equipment are undefined; silica NIR coverage is partial [S02 p.6].

### First feasibility experiment

1. Proposal: whey samples across the agreed protein range, from clear to turbid, with fat, casein and bubble variation.
2. Proposal: NIR transmission and reflection spectra with independent lab protein results and timing information.
3. Proposal: single-method models first; combine only if justified; validate on held-out batches or runs.
4. Proposal: compare prediction error, bias and ability to follow changes against requirements, separately for clear and turbid states.

**Decision basis.** Agree error, bias and response criteria with the client before testing; choose the simplest configuration meeting them on independent material.
