## 2. The challenge

Industrial customers often know **what** they need to monitor (concentration, reaction progress, contamination, moisture, product quality) but not **which** spectroscopy method, spectral range, fiber probe or measurement setup to test first.

**Build an AI assistant that helps an AFS expert make a first technical assessment of a new customer problem.** It must NOT replace the expert. It structures the first customer discussion with transparent, reviewable recommendations.

### For each customer scenario, the assistant must:
1. Extract the main process requirements and constraints.
2. Ask important clarification questions when information is missing.
3. Recommend one or more spectroscopy methods or combinations worth testing.
4. Suggest a high-level probe / measurement concept.
5. Explain the recommendation, limitations and uncertainty.
6. Propose a short first feasibility-study plan.

### Example
**Input:** "We need to monitor moisture and hydrocarbon contamination in soil. Measurements should be remote and the environment may be humid and dirty."
**Expected output:** clarification questions; recommended method(s); relevant spectral range; possible probe concept; main risks; a short plan for the first comparison experiment.

### Minimal requirements
- Accept a customer/process description in natural language.
- Use the supplied AFS knowledge base as the **main source** for technical recommendations.
- Recommend at least one spectroscopy approach and explain why.
- Ask clarification questions when key information is missing.
- Provide a simple feasibility-study plan.
- **Show the source / AFS material used for important recommendations.**
- Provide a working prototype that can be demonstrated.

### Hard rules (violating these could disqualify us)
- External sources are allowed, but AFS materials remain the main source. **Any external citation must include the quoted passage.**
- Any model or tool may be used.
- **Answers to H01–H06 must be generated by the assistant: no manual editing, no case-specific hard-coding.** They must be included in the repository.
- The jury may ask us to run a scenario or a **new enquiry live**, so the system must generalize, not just look good on the given cases.

### Evaluation criteria
| Criterion | What judges look for | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Technical quality | Recommendations are reasonable for the customer case | **30%** |
| Use of AFS knowledge | Recommendations grounded in supplied AFS materials | **20%** |
| Feasibility-study plan | First experiments are practical and useful | 15% |
| Clarification & uncertainty | Asks good questions; knows when info is insufficient | 15% |
| Explainability | Reasoning is clear, traceable, easy for an expert to review | 10% |
| Prototype & presentation | Works and is easy to demonstrate | 10% |

Note: 65% of the score is domain correctness + grounding + feasibility. A pretty UI matters least. Prioritize accuracy, grounding and citations.

### Required submission
- **Public Git repository** with source code, reproducible setup and setup instructions
- **Structured output for each of H01–H06:** requirements, questions, recommendation, risks, feasibility plan
- Short description of the **system architecture** and **how AFS materials are used**
- **PDF slides** + **demo video (max 5 minutes)**
- Live demo (up to 5 min) if requested

### Domain notes from the AFS opening presentation (verify against materials)
- AFS's core pitch: customers are pushed by vendors toward one method ("buy our NIR / buy our Raman"). AFS's **Multi-Spectral Fiber Systems** test **4 methods (Raman, FTIR/MIR, Fluorescence, NIR) across 0.3–16 µm** with robust fiber probes, then use chemometrics and data fusion to pick the best process-control solution.
- **AFS Guided Pathway:** (1) multi-method lab trials → (2) identify key spectral features → (3) choose a path: **A** chemometric model, **B** customized MSF system for R&D, **C** customized IoT spectral fiber sensors.
- **Probe types:** Diamond ATR, ATR-Loop, Raman, TransFlex, Reflex, Fluorescence, plus Combi-probes (e.g. ATR + Fluorescence/Raman in one probe; see Nature Sci. Rep. 14, 7430, 2024).
- **Fiber transmission ranges:** UV-VIS silica ~0.18–1.2 µm; VIS-NIR silica ~0.35–2.4 µm; chalcogenide ~1.1–6.5 µm; polycrystalline (AgHal/PIR) ~3–17 µm; hollow waveguides ~2–18 µm; fluoride fibers ~0.3–5.5 µm. **Method choice constrains fiber choice and therefore probe length/robustness.**
- **Spectral regions → analytes (µm):** 0.85–1.1 fat, protein, moisture; 1.3–2.2 moisture, polymers, APIs, ethanol; 3.0–4.5 hydrocarbons, CO₂; 6.0–10.5 ethanol, glucose, glutamine, organic acids, ammonium.
- **Architectures:** centralized process analyzer + multiplexer + long fibers (higher accuracy) vs. distributed spectral IoT sensors (lower installation cost).
- Other AFS topics: NIR-LED fiber moisture sensors (FlexiSens), IR-fiber-coupled ICL/QCL lasers for biomedical diagnostics.
